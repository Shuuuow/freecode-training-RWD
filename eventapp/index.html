Third Place Desider: France (🇫🇷) vs England (🇬🇧) 4-6, English Victory!!

What a bronze medal match! England has beaten France 6-4 in a wild, record-breaking thriller at Miami's Hard Rock Stadium. The Three Lions stormed to a 4-0 halftime lead—Declan Rice blasting one in from outside the box in the 3rd minute, Ezri Konsa heading home Rice's corner in the 18th, and Bukayo Saka scoring twice before the break. But France came roaring back. Kylian Mbappé scored in the 48th, set up Bradley Barcola six minutes later, then struck again in the 66th—his 10th goal of the tournament, taking him past Messi as the World Cup's all-time top scorer with 22 career goals. Suddenly it was 4-3. England steadied. Saka completed his hat-trick from an 87th-minute penalty—only the fourth England player ever to score a World Cup hat-trick. Ousmane Dembélé pulled one back in stoppage time, but Jude Bellingham sealed it moments later with England's sixth. This 10-goal epic is the highest-scoring World Cup match since 1982. England secures bronze—their best finish since lifting the trophy in 1966. France finishes fourth in Didier Deschamps' final match after 14 years in charge. Unforgettable.

Semi-finals:

Spain punched their ticket first with a dominant 2-0 shutout of France in Dallas. On the day after his 19th birthday, Lamine Yamal drew an early penalty—Mikel Oyarzabal converted in the 22nd minute. Pedro Porro doubled the lead in the 58th, while Spain's midfield of Rodri, Olmo, and Fabian Ruiz completely controlled the game. France, playing in Didier Deschamps' penultimate match as manager, never found their rhythm. The next night in Atlanta, defending champions Argentina staged a dramatic comeback against England. Anthony Gordon put England ahead in the 55th minute, but the Three Lions sat back and invited pressure. Lionel Messi orchestrated the turnaround—setting up Enzo Fernandez for the 85th-minute equalizer, then finding Lautaro Martinez for the stoppage-time winner. Argentina advanced to their seventh World Cup final. Spain and Argentina now meet in Sunday's all-Spanish-speaking final—the first since 1930. France and England face off in Saturday's third-place match in Miami.
